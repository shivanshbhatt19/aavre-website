Udaipur sightseeing in one day by cab: a route that works

By Aavre    August 2026    7 min read

Most people arrive in Udaipur with one full day for the city and a list of places pulled off the internet. The list is usually fine. The order is usually wrong, and the order is what decides whether the day feels like a holiday or an errand.

Udaipur is small. Almost everything worth seeing sits within twenty minutes of everything else. What is not small is the middle of the day in the old city, the walk from where a car can stop to where you actually want to be, and the queue at the palace gate at eleven in the morning. Plan around those three things and the same list turns into an easy day.

Here is the route we use when guests book a full day of local sightseeing, and why it runs in this order.

Start early, at the City Palace

The City Palace opens in the morning and it is the one place worth being early for. It is the largest palace complex in Rajasthan, built up over four centuries by successive Maharanas, and it takes two hours to walk properly. Arrive at opening and you get the courtyards with the light coming in low, the mirrored rooms without a crowd behind you, and the balcony views over Lake Pichola before the haze settles.

Arrive at eleven instead and you get the same palace with three tour groups in it. The difference is entirely in the hour you choose.

Give it two hours. If you are short of time, the museum route through the palace is the part to keep and the crystal gallery is the part to skip.

Walk out into the old city while it is still quiet

Come out of the palace and you are already at the top of the old city. Jagdish Temple is a short walk, up its steep flight of steps, and it is worth going in for the carving as much as the darshan. From there the lanes run down towards Gangaur Ghat, past the miniature painting shops, the silver, the tailors and the roof cafes.

This is the part of Udaipur people remember, and it is also the part a car cannot help you with. The lanes are narrow, many are one way, and the ones that are not are full of scooters. The right way to do it is to have the car drop you near the palace, walk the old city on foot at your own pace, and pick the car up again at a point on the outer road when you come out. Any driver who works this city knows those points without being asked.

If you want the classic view of the City Palace rising off the water, walk across to the far side of the lake near Ambrai Ghat. It is a five minute drive around and it is the photograph everyone comes home with. Late afternoon and just after sunset both work.

Late morning to midday, move north

By late morning the old city is warm and busy, and the north side of Udaipur opens up. This is the easy driving half of the day.

Saheliyon ki Bari, the garden built for the queen's companions, is a calm twenty minutes of fountains, lotus pools and marble elephants. Fateh Sagar is a few minutes further, and the lake road along it is the most pleasant stretch of tarmac in the city. If you have any interest in old machinery, the vintage car collection near Gulab Bagh is a genuine surprise, with the cars the royal family actually used, including the Rolls Royces from the palace's own garage.

Eat lunch here rather than in the old city. The parking is easier, the drive to the afternoon is shorter, and you are not fighting your way back out through the lanes at the hottest hour.

Keep the sunset free, then choose one

Udaipur gives you two good sunsets and one day only lets you have one of them. This is the single decision that shapes the whole itinerary, so make it in the morning rather than at five in the evening.

Sajjangarh, the Monsoon Palace

The white palace on the hill above the city, inside the Sajjangarh sanctuary, with the whole lake system laid out below it. The road up is a proper climb through the forest. Timings close in the early evening and the last entry is before that, so this is an afternoon plan rather than a late one. Aim to be starting up the hill by late afternoon, not at sunset itself.

A boat ride on Lake Pichola

The other one. Boats leave from the ghat below the City Palace, the last ones run into the golden hour, and the view of the ghats and the Lake Palace from the water is the reason people fall for this city. Seats fill up on good weather evenings, so it is worth asking about the last slot before you build the day around it.

Both are lovely. Neither is improved by rushing from one to the other, which is what happens when people try to do both. Choose in the morning and the evening arranges itself.

The evening: folk dance, ghats and dinner

The evening folk dance at Bagore ki Haveli, right on Gangaur Ghat, is the one organised evening in Udaipur that is genuinely worth booking. It runs daily in the early evening in a courtyard by the water, about an hour of Rajasthani dance and puppetry, and the ticket counter opens before the show for anyone who has not booked online. In season it sells out, so decide about it in the morning too.

After that, eat by the water. The roof restaurants around Gangaur Ghat and the far bank at Ambrai look straight across at the lit up palace. Your car cannot reach most of them, and it does not need to. Get dropped at the ghat, walk in, and have the pickup arranged for when you come out.

The rough shape of a good day: palace at opening, old city on foot until late morning, gardens and lakes and lunch through the middle, Sajjangarh or a boat ride in the late afternoon, folk dance and dinner by the water in the evening. About ten hours, most of it walking, with the car doing the joins.

What to leave out

The temptation in a one day itinerary is to add Eklingji, Nathdwara or Haldighati to the end of it. Do not. Each is a half day of its own once you count the drive and the temple timings, and stapling one onto a city day means seeing the city badly and the temple worse. If you have a second day, that is what the second day is for.

Shilpgram, the crafts village, is the other common add on. It is genuinely good, but it is an hour you have to take from somewhere else, and in one day it usually comes out of the old city, which is a poor trade.

Season changes the day, not the route

From October to March the order above works exactly as written, and you can be out through the middle of the day without thinking about it. In April, May and June, start earlier, take a longer lunch, and put the outdoor half of the day at the two ends. Through the monsoon the lakes are full and the hills are green, which is the best the city ever looks, and the only real change is keeping the Sajjangarh climb flexible on a heavy rain day. We wrote about that season in more detail in our Udaipur monsoon guide.

Why a car and driver, and not rides one at a time

A sightseeing day is not one journey. It is six or seven short ones, with unpredictable gaps between them, most of them starting from a place where a car cannot wait.

Booked ride by ride, every one of those gaps becomes a small negotiation in the sun, with your shopping in your hands and nowhere to leave a jacket. Booked as a full day, the car is a base. Bags stay in it, the air conditioning is running when you come back to it, and the driver is at the pickup point before you are, because he already knows which one you will come out of.

The other quiet advantage is local knowledge. Where to be dropped for Jagdish Temple, which side of Fateh Sagar to drive at what hour, when to leave for the hill so you are not behind a line of cars at the gate. None of it is on a map and all of it is the difference between eight relaxed hours and ten tiring ones.

Common questions

Can you really see Udaipur in one day?

Yes. The main sights sit within a short drive of each other, so one full day covers the City Palace, the old city and its ghats, the gardens and lakes on the north side, and either a sunset at Sajjangarh or a boat ride on Lake Pichola. What one day does not allow is both sunsets, or the outstation temples and forts, which need days of their own.

What is the best order to see Udaipur in?

Start at the City Palace when it opens, walk the old city and Jagdish Temple while the lanes are quiet, move to the gardens and lakes on the north side around midday, and keep the sunset for Sajjangarh or a boat ride. The order matters more than the list, because the old city is hardest in the middle of the day and the best light comes at the end of it.

Can a car reach the old city and Jagdish Temple?

Not all the way. The lanes around Jagdish Temple and Gangaur Ghat are narrow and often one way, so a car drops you at the edge and you walk in. A driver who knows the city picks the right drop point and waits somewhere you can reach easily when you come out.

How many hours do you need?

About ten hours from a morning start to the end of the evening if you want the palace, the old city, the lakes and a sunset without rushing. A half day covers the City Palace and the old city, or the lakes and Sajjangarh, but not both.

Is a full day cab better than booking rides one at a time?

For a sightseeing day, yes. Your bags stay in the car, you are not arranging a new ride every ninety minutes, and the driver is already at the pickup point when you come out. Booking one ride at a time works in a city with plenty of waiting cars, and the old city is not that place.
