Why we run electric cabs in Udaipur

By Aavre    September 2026    6 min read

People notice it about ninety seconds into the first ride, usually somewhere on the road out of the airport. The conversation in the back seat is happening at a normal volume. Nobody is leaning forward to be heard. There is no engine underneath the talking.

That is the reason we run electric cars, and it is worth being straight about the order of things. We did not build Aavre around electric vehicles and then work out how to sell them. We wanted to run the most comfortable cab in Udaipur, and after driving these roads for years, the quiet car turned out to be the answer.

What quiet actually buys you

A cab journey in Rajasthan is rarely short. The run to Kumbhalgarh is a couple of hours of Aravalli road each way. Chittorgarh is a highway morning. Even a city sightseeing day adds up to hours in a seat.

Over that length of time, engine noise is not a detail. It is the thing that makes a family stop talking, that wakes a child who finally fell asleep after the fort, that turns a work call from the back seat into an apology. Take it away and the drive stops being the part of the day you endure between the places you came to see. People arrive at Ranakpur having had a conversation, rather than having sat through a journey.

The second thing is the vibration, or the absence of it. There is no shudder at idle at a level crossing, no engine note rising and falling on a climb, no diesel smell drifting into the cabin in slow traffic in the old city. On the hill roads it is more noticeable still. An electric motor gives its pull from a standstill without gear changes, so the long climbs to Kumbhalgarh and up to Sajjangarh are steady and smooth instead of a series of downshifts.

The one place guests are unanimous is the wait. On a sightseeing day the car sits idle for long stretches while you are inside a palace or a temple, and in a Rajasthan summer that normally means a driver running an engine for the air conditioning, or a hot car when you return. An electric car simply stays cool, with no engine running and nothing to smell when you open the door.

The part that is our problem, not yours

Being honest about this matters more than being enthusiastic about it. An electric fleet is harder to run than a diesel one, and every bit of that difficulty sits on our side of the arrangement.

A diesel taxi can take a booking at any hour and solve the fuel question in five minutes at a pump. We cannot. Every route we sell has been driven and measured, the round trip range is worked out with the air conditioning on and a full car of guests and luggage, and cars are charged at base between bookings rather than in the middle of somebody's day. That is why we plan routes carefully and why we hold a car back rather than stretch it.

What that means for a guest is simple. You should never hear about charging at all. If a route ever genuinely needs a stop, we say so before you book, not after you have set off. And if we cannot do a journey properly, we say that too, which is a better answer than finding out on a hill road at four in the afternoon.

Charging in Udaipur, honestly

Udaipur is not Delhi. The public charging network here is thin and it is the reason most operators in the city have not gone this way. We charge at our own base, which is what makes the model work today, and we treat public chargers as a convenience rather than a plan.

That is changing, though slowly. Rajasthan has been sanctioned funding for several hundred public charging points under the national PM e-Drive scheme, with dozens of locations earmarked for Udaipur alone, alongside chargers along the highway stretches that our day trip routes run on. When those arrive, the routes we can offer comfortably get longer. Until then we would rather be conservative about what we promise than optimistic about what we can deliver.

Where an electric cab is genuinely the better car

Airport transfers, city sightseeing, the half day run to Eklingji and Nathdwara, and the full day trips to Kumbhalgarh, Ranakpur and Chittorgarh are all comfortably within range as complete round trips, and all of them are improved by a quiet car. Those are the journeys we built the fleet around.

Long one way intercity runs are where an electric car asks more of the planning, and we quote those individually rather than pretending the answer is the same for every route. Tell us the journey and we will tell you plainly whether it is a good fit.

What it does for the fare

There is a commercial reason too, and it happens to line up with the promise we make on every booking.

Our fares are fixed. The same at midnight as at midday, the same in December as in July, with no surge and nothing to negotiate at either end of the ride. That is easy to say and harder to hold when your largest running cost moves with the fuel price and your busiest season is also your most expensive one. Electricity is steadier than diesel, and a steadier cost base is part of what lets us publish a fare and then keep it. You can see current fares on our services page.

A lake city, and a small piece of arithmetic

We are not going to overstate this part. One small fleet does not change the air in a city.

But Udaipur is a place people come to for the quiet of the water at seven in the morning and the light on the ghats in the evening, and the traffic that carries them there is working against the thing they came for. A cab that adds nothing to the noise on the ghat road, and nothing to the haze over the lakes, is a small alignment between what the city is and how visitors move through it. In a town this size, that is worth something even before it is worth anything.

What has not changed

The car is the means, not the point. Every cab is 5 star safety rated with 6 airbags, a dashcam and GPS on every ride, and each cab sits up to 4 guests. Bookings are pre confirmed and direct on WhatsApp, with no app in between and no middleman taking a cut of the fare. Drivers are professional, know the routes and the timings, and are not paid to sell you a detour to a handicraft emporium.

The electric part is how the ride feels. Everything else is how the service works, and that was the promise first.

Common questions

Will an electric cab complete a full day trip?

Yes. Kumbhalgarh, Ranakpur, Chittorgarh, Nathdwara and a full day of city sightseeing are all planned as complete round trips, with the range worked out before the car leaves. The planning happens at our end, not in the middle of your day.

Will I have to wait while it charges?

No. Cars are charged at base between bookings, and routes are planned so a charging stop is not part of your day. If a route ever genuinely needs one, we tell you before you book rather than after you set off.

Is an electric car slower on the hill roads?

The opposite. An electric motor delivers its pull from a standstill with no gear changes, which is exactly what the climbs to Kumbhalgarh and Sajjangarh ask for. No downshifting, no engine noise building, no hunting for power on a gradient.

Does the air conditioning affect the trip?

It uses charge, and we plan for it. The air conditioning stays on, including while the car waits for you, which in a Rajasthan summer is one of the real advantages of an electric car. There is no idling engine, so a cool car costs nothing in noise or fumes.

Does an electric cab cost more to book?

No. Our fares are fixed and published, the same at any hour and any time of year. Running costs are steadier than fuel prices, which is part of how we hold a flat fare through the season instead of moving it with demand.
